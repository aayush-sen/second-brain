# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Aayush, solo — the only user of his Agentic OS dashboard. Checked multiple times a day, not for one deep sit-down but for a running mental model of where career pipeline, ventures, projects, outreach, and deadlines stand. Occasionally screen-shared with a mentor or collaborator, so it needs to read cleanly to a second viewer too, not just to Aayush mid-triage.

## Product Purpose

A real-time mirror over `os/` — the pipeline, ventures, projects, outreach, deadlines, and memory feed that make up Aayush's agentic OS — surfaced in one page so he can keep situational awareness without opening individual files, plus act directly on what he sees (drag a pipeline card, approve an outbox draft, close a deadline, log a note) without leaving it. Success is not just clearing a queue fast; it's always knowing where everything stands.

## Positioning

One glanceable command center: everything across the agentic OS that needs attention or context is surfaced in one place, nothing to hunt for across separate collections or files.

## Brand Personality

Calm, spacious, considered. Streamlined and legible over dense and terminal-like — the restrained, typography-led feel of Hermes-style agent dashboards, which the v3 build (2026-07-10) adopts outright: one cream ink on deep teal, Mondwest display type, Courier Prime readouts. Personality comes through clean type and deliberate spacing, not decoration.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS admin-template look — default card-grid dashboard kits. Overly artsy or decorative flourishes for their own sake. Cramped, terminal-dense layouts that trade breathing room for raw information density.

## Design Principles

- Breathing room over density — legible spacing beats cramming more into the viewport.
- Everything actionable is one click away — no gap between seeing state and acting on it.
- Built for a glance, holds up under a stare — works for a 5-second check-in and a slower review alike.
- Typography carries hierarchy; color-coded borders on every element are a crutch, not a system.
- Second-viewer legible — should read cleanly if screen-shared, without needing the density stripped out first.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast minimum throughout. Any animation needs a reduced-motion fallback. No dedicated colorblind-safe palette requirement beyond that, but status signaling shouldn't lean on left-border hue alone.
